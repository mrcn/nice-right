# Understand competitor / adjacent pattern audit

Date: 2026-06-23
Kanban card: `t_46aed565`

## Sources checked

- DummyReader — `dummyreader.com`
- Speechify — `speechify.com`
- NaturalReader — `naturalreaders.com`
- ElevenReader — `elevenreader.io`

## Executive read

The market is crowded around **read anything aloud** and **natural voices**. Understand should not fight that head-on.

The open lane is:

> **hard text → listenable retelling → trust check → phone listening**

This is different from:

- generic TTS
- audiobook libraries
- voiceover creation
- AI summaries
- document Q&A

## DummyReader

### What it does well

- Strong transformation framing: any book/text becomes clearer and listenable.
- Uses concrete before/after examples.
- Focuses on hard texts: archaic, foreign-language, technical, academic.
- Makes long-form transformation feel plausible with audio-length references.

### What to borrow

- before/after proof
- long-text ambition
- source categories that prove the point
- “not just snippets” framing

### What to avoid

- overclaiming “any book, any length, any language” before the product can support it
- making translation/modernization/simplification feel like one undifferentiated magic act
- unclear trust boundaries

## Speechify

### Pattern

Speechify is a massive voice AI productivity platform. It competes on:

- read anything aloud
- PDFs/articles/books
- cross-device listening
- AI summaries
- Q&A
- voice typing
- scale/social proof

### Implication for Understand

Do not compete as “another Speechify.” Instead:

> Speechify reads documents aloud. Understand makes difficult text easier to keep listening to first.

Speechify owns broad utility. Understand needs depth around difficult text.

## NaturalReader

### Pattern

NaturalReader emphasizes:

- natural-sounding voices
- reading/accessibility
- voiceover creation
- education
- many languages
- enterprise trust/security
- personal/commercial/EDU segmentation

### Implication for Understand

NaturalReader is voice-quality and accessibility broad-market TTS. Understand’s distinction should be transformation and trust:

> clearer retelling + closeness + compare with original

## ElevenReader

### Pattern

ElevenReader emphasizes:

- “listen to anything in voices you’ll love”
- audiobooks and included library
- PDFs/articles/docs/web novels
- high-quality voices
- soundscapes and narrator choice
- cross-device listening

### Implication for Understand

ElevenReader owns delightful voices/library. Understand should not lead with voice alone. Lead with:

> on-ramp to hard text

Then use voice as performance layer.

## Strategic rules from audit

1. Do not lead with generic “text to speech app.”
2. Do not lead with “best voices.”
3. Do not imply we replace Speechify/NaturalReader/ElevenReader broadly.
4. Lead with difficult-text transformation.
5. Show original → Understand → trust notes.
6. Make mobile listening the habit layer, not the only differentiation.
7. Keep examples useful and source-attributed.
8. Use comparison pages later, after core pages are credible.

## Messaging contrast table

| Competitor type | Dominant promise | Understand counter-position |
|---|---|---|
| Generic TTS | Read anything aloud | Make difficult text listenable first |
| AI voice app | Better voices | Better on-ramp to the text |
| Audiobook/library app | More books/content | Bring your hard text and transform it |
| Summarizer | Shorten the text | Keep the text experience, but make it easier to hear |
| Document Q&A | Ask questions about docs | Continue through the text as audio |

## Site implications

The own-domain site should emphasize:

- category creation
- How It Works
- examples/proof
- use cases
- trust/comparison model
- testing CTA

Example audio remains useful, but it should not become the entire footprint.
